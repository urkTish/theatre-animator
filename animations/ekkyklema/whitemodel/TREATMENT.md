# Ekkyklema · Projection on White Model: treatment (15.0 s sample)

Style: lemo-opuscar **Projection on White Model** (`styles/white-model/STYLE.md`), applied to our own reconstruction (geometry and motion from `../motion/`). No narration: captions and projected readouts carry the film, as `shared/STYLE.md` asks (silent-capable). Sound design only.

## Three candidate structures

1. **A question answered layer by layer.** House lights, lights out, then three projected layers: *the door* (size, inward swing) → *the roll* (path, distance, counter) → *how sure are we* (evidence pins). The roll itself is the turn. Ends with the house light coming back on the tableau.
2. **Before / after on one take.** The model lit twice: first the closed façade (the "problem": what happens inside is unseen), then the same view after the roll with the result revealed. Strong idea, but 14 s is too short for two holds plus reading.
3. **One object's journey.** Follow only the platform: a pick-out on it in the dark interior, then the travel measured A→B along the side. Clean, but loses the façade and the door facts.

**Chosen: 1**, with the platform pick-out of 3 folded into the opening of the roll. It puts every required fact on screen in the order the machine uses them (door first, then the roll), and its turn is a physical event, not a caption.

## Logline and arc

A white museum model of the wooden skene sits in a dark room; a projector shows, one layer at a time, how the doors open inward, how far the platform rolls, and how sure we are of each part.

- Setup (0–2 s): the model as an object; house light; the room goes dark, the lamp strikes.
- Turn (2–10 s): door measured, leaves swing inward (the swing arcs are projected as plan-drawing quadrants on the floor), then the platform rolls out along a projected lane while a counter runs 0.00 → 2.93 m.
- Ending (10–15 s): three evidence pins, in the evidence code; the house light comes back up and the camera settles at audience eye height on the tableau.

## Benchmark (grammar only)

- Projection-mapped planning models in museum galleries (dark room, soft throw, colour only where light lands); architectural plan drawing (door-swing quadrants, dimension lines with ticks).
- What to learn: one layer at a time, the measured line growing A→B, the object never changing.
- What not to take: no layout, typeface, colour scheme or music from any of them.

Two near-silences: between the relay and the lamp strike (1.5–1.8 s) and a dip of beds and music just before the roll starts (5.5–5.8 s); the first sound after it is the wheels.

## Type

Identity label top-left (place · date · model scale · source), readout in the left third (index + layer name, one figure in Inter Tight 200, one label, one source line), pins on dark plates with a hairline to an anchor on the model, all through one world → screen function. Greek in GFS Didot. Monospace for numbers: IBM Plex Mono.

## Changes after the look rounds

- The lead-in grew from 2.4 to 2.8 s (film 15.0 s) so readout 01 can be read in full before the roll; the scene's own timeline is untouched.
- The subject is framed centre-right throughout so the left-third readouts never cover it; the roll's measured line runs on the lane's left side, clear of the standing actor.
- The attested layer became a solid projected arrow in front of the platform (the lane itself ends up under the platform and was hidden).

## Not the demo again (DIRECTOR.md §11)

Compared with the library's Rotterdam demo: structure (a machine's single action measured, not a city's growth by decade), opening (a high wide crane down onto the whole object, not a macro glide), signature shot (the roll along a projected lane with a counter and a hidden-line ruler, not a shadow range line), camera path (front-right crane → orbit → eye-level settle, not orbits over a city), sound (sound design, no score), ending (house light up with the evidence layer left on alone at audience eye height, no end card) all differ. Shared on purpose, as the style's grammar: lights out / lamp on.
