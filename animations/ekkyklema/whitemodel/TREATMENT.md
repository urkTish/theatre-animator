# Ekkyklema · Projection on White Model: treatment (14.5 s sample)

Style: lemo-opuscar **Projection on White Model** (`styles/white-model/STYLE.md`), applied to our own reconstruction (geometry and motion from `../motion/`). No narration: captions and projected readouts carry the film, as `shared/STYLE.md` asks (silent-capable). Sound design only.

## Three candidate structures

1. **A question answered layer by layer.** House lights, lights out, then three projected layers: *the door* (size, inward swing) → *the roll* (path, distance, counter) → *how sure are we* (evidence pins). The roll itself is the turn. Ends with the house light coming back on the tableau.
2. **Before / after on one take.** The model lit twice: first the closed façade (the "problem": what happens inside is unseen), then the same view after the roll with the result revealed. Strong idea, but 14 s is too short for two holds plus reading.
3. **One object's journey.** Follow only the platform: a pick-out on it in the dark interior, then the travel measured A→B along the side. Clean, but loses the façade and the door facts.

**Chosen: 1**, with the platform pick-out of 3 folded into the opening of the roll. It puts every required fact on screen in the order the machine uses them (door first, then the roll), and its turn is a physical event, not a caption.

## Logline and arc

A white museum model of the wooden skene sits in a dark room; a projector shows, one layer at a time, how the doors open inward, how far the platform rolls, and how sure we are of each part.

- Setup (0–3 s): the model as an object; house light; the room goes dark, the lamp strikes.
- Turn (3–9 s): door measured, leaves swing inward (the swing arcs are projected as plan-drawing quadrants on the floor), then the platform rolls out along a projected lane while a counter runs 0.00 → 2.93 m.
- Ending (9–14.5 s): three evidence pins, in the evidence code; the house light comes back up and the camera settles at audience eye height on the tableau.

## Benchmark (grammar only)

- Projection-mapped planning models in museum galleries (dark room, soft throw, colour only where light lands); architectural plan drawing (door-swing quadrants, dimension lines with ticks).
- What to learn: one layer at a time, the measured line growing A→B, the object never changing.
- What not to take: no layout, typeface, colour scheme or music from any of them.
