# Ekkyklema — sources and findings (Stage 2)

How sure each point is (the same tags drive the visual style; see `shared/STYLE.md`):
- **[A] Attested:** stated in an ancient text that we read in the original.
- **[I] Inferred:** a scholarly inference with an argued basis.
- **[R] Reconstruction:** a choice the animation has to make, with weak or no evidence.

How each source was checked: ✔ read by us in full or in the passage itself; ◐ known only through another scholar's citation; ○ known only from a search snippet. The ○ items still need checking before anything rests on them.

## 3. Findings, by research question

1. **Mechanism.** A low wheeled platform, rolled out through the door and back in. **[A]** for rolling out and in (Aristophanes' verbs); **[I]** for the wheels (scholia; the consensus).
   - The turntable or pivot theory (Exon 1900, Flickinger) rests on the scholia's verb *strephō*, and no ruin shows a pivot. It is now a minority view.
   - Casanova reads *strephō* as a crank-turned windlass that pulls the trolley out. That is argued but unproven: **[R]**.
2. **Size.** No 5th-century door survives, because the skene was wooden.
   - Estimates run from about 2.5 × 1.3 m (Dearden) to about 3 m wide (Haigh, from Eretria). **[R]**
   - Capacity: the heaviest load usually assigned is *Heracles* 1028ff., Heracles bound to a column among four bodies, so a few figures and props. It is generally doubted that it could carry a whole chorus. **[I]**
3. **Typical use.** It reveals the result of an indoor event, usually bodies or a disturbed hero, as a tableau.
   - Secure: *Acharnians*, *Thesmophoriazusae* **[A]**.
   - Widely agreed: *Ajax* 346, *Hippolytus* 808, *Heracles* 1028, *Electra* 1458 **[I]**.
   - Disputed: *Agamemnon* 1372, *Choephori* 973, *Eumenides* 64, *Clouds* 184.
4. **First use.** Divided.
   - Certain by 425 BC: *Acharnians* parodies it as familiar **[A]**.
   - In the *Oresteia* (458): affirmed by Haigh, Wiles and Casanova; denied by Taplin and Hammond. **[I]**, contested.
5. **Setting.** A wooden skene, introduced about 460–458. It uses the central door, whether there was one door or three. A raised stage is doubtful for tragedy; comic vases show a low stage with steps. **[I]**
6. **Movement.** Done by unseen helpers ("someone roll me in", *Thesm.* 265) **[A]**.
   - Pushed or pulled by stagehands is the default **[I]**; the windlass is an alternative **[R]**.
   - Grooved tracks survive at Eretria, but they are Hellenistic and not evidence for Athens.
7. **Visual evidence.** No ancient image securely shows the machine.
   - For the Ajax pose, two later descriptions survive: Timomachus' lost painting, and a small scene on the Tabula Iliaca Capitolina showing Ajax seated in dejection beside a ram's head. Both are in Jebb's Introduction §20.
   - Old reconstruction drawings (Exon, Flickinger) show theories that most scholars now reject.
